    How to read this

    The bars compare each sector to the Nasdaq-100 over the past month. Bar to the right = beating the market (money flowing in). Bar to the left = trailing it (money flowing out). The line under each bar is this week’s turn — a sector deep in the red but green this week may be waking up.

    The two ladders answer two questions: the first shows money moving within the AI trade; the second shows where money goes when it leaves AI — banks, healthcare, oil, gold, and other old-economy destinations, measured on the same scale.

    “What kind of move is this” runs style groups head-to-head — small vs large caps, high-beta vs low-vol, junk vs quality credit, dividend payers, bonds, gold. When the risky side is winning, sector weakness is rotation you can trade; when the defensive side is winning, it’s de-risking and dips deserve caution. Each sector also shows the character of its companies (typical size, dividend yield, beta) and, where a clean ETF exists, real fund flows — the 5-day change in that ETF’s shares outstanding, which only moves when money actually enters or leaves the fund.

    The little dots show how many of each sector’s stocks are still in an uptrend (above their 50-day average price). Mostly filled = healthy. Mostly hollow = damaged.

    Tap any sector to see its stocks. “Setup score” (0–100) rates how close a stock is to a buyable recovery — 70+ is interesting.
